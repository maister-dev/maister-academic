# Judging a source

Fast checks, in the order that eliminates the most bad sources per second.

## Is it dated?

No visible publication or revision date → treat as unusable for a volatile
claim. This single check removes most of the bad corpus.

## Does it version itself?

Documentation that never names the version it documents cannot support a
version-pinned claim. Look for a version selector, a release tag, a spec
revision line.

## Is it the origin or a retelling?

Follow the citation chain to its end. A page that says "according to the
documentation" is a pointer to the source, not the source. Retellings drift:
each hop drops a qualifier.

## Does it have something to sell?

A vendor documenting its own product is primary for *what the product does* and
worthless for *how it compares*. Keep those two uses apart.

## Would it survive a student checking it?

Students in a course about a fast-moving field will open your links. A dead
link, a redirect to a marketing homepage, or a page that now says the opposite
costs the course more credibility than the claim was worth.

## Community sources

An issue thread proves a problem was reported. It does not prove the problem
exists now, was real then, or was fixed. Cite it as evidence of the report, and
say so in `notes`.
